Case study 01 · Sustainable packaging

Biobased films engineered for real processing.

Connecting polyester synthesis, crystallization, blown-film extrusion, structural analysis, and end-use performance to evaluate PPeAT and PDDF as sustainable flexible-packaging materials.

The challenge

Performance without abandoning processability.

Commercial PBAT offers biodegradability and film processability but its low stiffness often requires blending or additional material. The project examined whether partially or fully biobased polyesters could provide stronger mechanical and barrier performance while remaining compatible with conventional film blowing.

My role

I integrated polymer synthesis, formulation, nucleation, rheology, extrusion, mechanical testing, gas and water-vapor permeation, and X-ray scattering to connect molecular structure with film-processing behavior and application performance.

Approach

  • Prepared PPeAT and PDDF alongside commercial PBAT and LLDPE benchmarks.
  • Produced continuous blown films and compared machine and transverse directions.
  • Measured tensile, dynamic mechanical, oxygen, carbon-dioxide, and water-vapor performance.
  • Used WAXS and SAXS to connect crystallinity and orientation with measured properties.
