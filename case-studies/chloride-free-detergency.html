Case study 05 · Industry collaboration

Expanding the design space for chloride-free cleaning.

A Church & Dwight-funded study combined detergency experiments, HLD formulation science, phase behavior, and interfacial-tension measurements to understand how potassium carbonate changes optimal cleaning conditions.

The challenge

Translate electrolyte substitution into reliable cleaning performance.

Replacing chloride salts changes phase behavior, surfactant partitioning, and the relationship between interfacial tension and detergency. The study needed to determine whether potassium carbonate could provide effective cleaning while establishing a formulation window that product developers could use.

My role

I led the experimental work with the academic and industrial team, comparing three nonionic surfactants in carbonate- and chloride-containing systems. I connected detergency, phase behavior, HLD, and spinning-drop interfacial-tension results, then translated the findings into recommendations for the sponsor.

Approach

  • Screened narrow- and broad-range alcohol ethoxylates across controlled HLD conditions.
  • Measured removal of tripalmitin model soil from two substrate types.
  • Compared potassium carbonate with chloride-based reference formulations.
  • Used test-tube phase behavior and spinning-drop IFT as complementary measurements.
