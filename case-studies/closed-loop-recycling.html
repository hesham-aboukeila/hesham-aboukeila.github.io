Case study 03 · Circular materials

Returning a polyester to high-quality material.

Amine-catalyzed methanolysis, monomer recovery, and repolymerization established a closed-loop pathway for PPeAT60 while preserving its molecular structure, thermal stability, and modulus.

The challenge

Biodegradable should not mean single-use.

PPeAT60 was designed for flexible-film performance, but circularity also requires a technically credible end-of-life pathway. The study tested whether the polymer could be depolymerized, its monomers recovered, and the material rebuilt without losing the properties that made it useful.

My role

I evaluated the complete material-recovery loop by characterizing virgin and repolymerized PPeAT60 across molecular, thermal, crystalline, mechanical, rheological, and barrier properties.

Approach

  • Depolymerized PPeAT60 at 190°C for 3 hours using methanol and dimethylethylamine.
  • Recovered dimethyl terephthalate, dimethyl adipate, and 1,5-pentanediol, then restored the target monomer ratio.
  • Repolymerized the recovered stream through esterification and polycondensation.
  • Compared SEC, NMR, DSC, TGA, WAXS, tensile, rheology, and permeation results with virgin PPeAT60.

Important qualification: Ultimate elongation and barrier performance declined after repolymerization. The paper attributes these differences to trace impurities introduced during processing and identifies improved monomer purification and multi-cycle testing as the next development steps.
