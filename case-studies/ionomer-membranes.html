Case study 06 · Carbon capture

Engineering ionomer membranes for CO₂ separation.

A three-study research program connecting operating humidity, CO₂-philic additives, and nanostructure control to improve gas transport in ionomer and hybrid polymer membranes.

The research question

How can water become a design tool?

Polymer membranes must balance permeability, selectivity, mechanical integrity, and realistic operating conditions. Across three related studies, I investigated how water interacts with ionic domains and how membrane chemistry and processing can turn that interaction into higher CO₂ transport.

My role

My work spanned membrane preparation and characterization, gas-permeation testing, and analysis of how composition, temperature, relative humidity, casting history, and nanostructure influence separation performance.

Program scope

  • Measured gas transport under dry and humid operating conditions.
  • Evaluated CO₂-philic PEGDME additives in Nafion hybrid membranes.
  • Connected solvent casting and water conditioning to ionic-domain morphology.
  • Applied transport modeling and structure-property analysis to interpret performance.

One question · Three design levers

A connected research progression

Each study isolated a different lever while building toward the same objective: faster, selective CO₂ transport.

Chapter 01

Operating environment

Humidity activates transport in Aquivion.

Aquivion is a short-side-chain perfluorosulfonic acid ionomer with hydrophilic ionic domains. Gas-permeation measurements across temperature and relative-humidity conditions showed that absorbed water swells those domains and increases permeability.

The important result was not simply higher transport. Permeability increased by more than one order of magnitude while the membrane retained its ideal separation ability, demonstrating that humid operation could raise productivity without the selectivity loss commonly associated with polymer swelling.

Design lessonOperating humidity is not only a disturbance variable. In ionomers, it can be used deliberately to activate connected transport pathways.

Chapter 02

Material formulation

CO₂-philic additives create preferential pathways.

PEGDME with molecular weights of approximately 250 and 500 g/mol was incorporated into Nafion to increase CO₂ affinity and reorganize the ionic domains. TGA, FTIR, XRD, and SEM were combined with mixed-gas permeation to connect composition and morphology with transport.

At 40 wt% PEGDME 250, dry-state CO₂ permeability reached 57.4 Barrer, 36 times that of pristine Nafion. Under fully saturated conditions, permeability reached 446 Barrer with a CO₂/N₂ selectivity of 31. Higher loading was not automatically better because excessive PEGDME produced microphase separation and defects.

Design lessonAdditive identity and loading must be balanced. Connected CO₂-philic domains improve transport, while uncontrolled phase separation sacrifices selectivity.

Chapter 03

Processing and nanostructure

Water conditioning reorganizes the membrane.

Midblock-sulfonated multiblock polymer membranes were cast from solvents of different polarity, creating distinct nanoscale morphologies. SAXS, electron microscopy, water-vapor uptake, and mixed-gas permeation were used to determine how those structures responded to humidification and liquid-water conditioning.

Submersion in water followed by drying connected and reorganized the ionic microdomains. Under humid mixed-gas operation, conditioned membranes reached 482 Barrer CO₂ permeability and CO₂/N₂ selectivity as high as 61. Two formulations surpassed the Robeson upper bound.

Design lessonMembrane processing history can be as important as polymer chemistry because it controls whether ionic domains remain isolated or form continuous transport pathways.

Research synthesis

Three studies, three transferable design rules.

01

Control the environment

Humidity can activate ionic domains and increase membrane productivity without automatically reducing selectivity.

02

Engineer the formulation

CO₂-philic additives create fast transport pathways when composition and morphology remain controlled.

03

Use processing as a lever

Casting and water conditioning can reorganize nanostructure and move performance beyond conventional trade-offs.

Technical scope: Together, these studies connect membrane chemistry, operating conditions, morphology, and gas transport across Aquivion, Nafion/PEGDME hybrids, and a sulfonated multiblock polymer. All quantitative results shown above are taken from the linked peer-reviewed publications.
