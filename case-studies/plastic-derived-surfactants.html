Case study 04 · Plastics upcycling

Plastic waste redesigned as biodegradable surfactants.

A multi-institution pathway transformed post-consumer recycled HDPE into high-purity carboxylate surfactants, then tested whether the resulting molecules could compete with conventional anionic benchmarks.

The challenge

Move recycled polyethylene into a higher-value application.

Mechanical recycling often returns polyethylene to lower-value uses. This collaboration investigated whether real post-consumer HDPE could instead supply surfactant-range molecules with the purity and performance needed for cleaning and formulation applications.

My role

Within the multi-institution team, I focused on physicochemical and application-relevant characterization of the plastic-derived carboxylate surfactants. I measured critical micelle concentration, surface and interfacial behavior, foam performance, calcium tolerance, and solubility-related behavior against conventional sodium carboxylate benchmarks.

Approach

  • Benchmarked the plastic-derived mixture against sodium decanoate and sodium laurate.
  • Used surface-tension measurements to determine CMC and surface activity.
  • Evaluated foam formation, drainage, and stability using dynamic foam analysis.
  • Tested calcium-ion tolerance to assess performance under hard-water conditions.

What changed

Waste carbon became a credible performance ingredient.

0.08 wt%

Low CMC

The plastic-derived mixture formed micelles at a low concentration and showed higher surface activity than the two linear-carboxylate references.

Competitive

Practical surfactant behavior

Foam stability, surface activity, and divalent-ion tolerance were comparable to conventional anionic surfactants.

Circular

Higher-value pathway

The work demonstrated an application route from real PCR-HDPE to biodegradable specialty chemicals rather than lower-value recycled plastic.
